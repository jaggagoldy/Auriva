APS-009 · Doctor Workspace Blueprint · Approved with refinements

The Doctor Workspace.

The complete Doctor experience for Auriva — from a phone number on a landing page to the ten-thousandth consult. This blueprint reconciles the approved IA (DOC-001–014, APS-005), the shipped Live Queue and Consult Workbench code, the AUTH-005 onboarding draft, and the Organization multi-affiliation seam into one implementable design. Where a screen needs a backend that doesn't exist yet, it says so — nothing here pretends. Revision 2 incorporates product review: Today renamed from "Dashboard" and enriched into Mission Control (D5), Patients and Practice made faceted/sub-sectioned (D4/D8), Profile rebuilt around Personal/Professional/Practice/Verification with a reserved slot for a future Doctor Reputation score (D7), and all seven open questions resolved (§02).

01 · Ground Truth

What already exists, and what gates what

This is not a greenfield design. Five things are fixed inputs; one register lists every backend gap so no screen in this document quietly assumes capabilities that aren't built. Engineering should read every later section as a diff against this baseline.

FIXED 1

Design system is live code

Auriva Teal #0D9488, slate neutrals, Inter, 6/8/12/16 radii, fixed status-chip palette (dot + label, never color alone) — already implemented in src/app/globals.css and src/shared/queue.ts STATUS_META. This doc introduces zero new tokens.

FIXED 2

Screen IDs are permanent

DOC-001…014 (APS-005) are kept as-is — extended, never renamed. Two scope amendments are proposed explicitly in D4; everything else maps 1:1.

FIXED 3

DOC-001 / DOC-002 are shipped

live-queue.tsx, queue-sidebar.tsx, consultation-pane.tsx work today against the real 8-state appointment machine. D5 and D6 are critique-and-refine passes with explicit KEEP / CHANGE / ADD verdicts — not redesigns.

FIXED 4

Onboarding has a draft

AUTH-005 exists as a 3-step design in patient-doctor-flow-mockup.html (identity → qualifications & clinic → review/pending), entered via the unified phone+OTP door. D2 extends it; it does not replace it.

FIXED 5

Multi-org has a domain seam

src/domain/organization.ts defines Organization / OrganizationMember (mapped onto Clinic / StaffProfile today, real tables later). D8 designs practice management against this seam so the UI survives the schema migration untouched.

RULE

No fake UI, anywhere

Every control in this blueprint either works against a real capability or is an honest "Coming soon" state — the same pattern as the Patient app's Labs/Pharmacy tabs. The register below is the single source of truth for which is which.

Deliverable 1 · Persona

Who the workspace serves

One workspace, three postures of the same profession. The primary persona is the Indian OPD clinician — high volume, tiny consult windows, zero tolerance for software that costs seconds. The other two personas stress the same screens differently (multi-context switching, org-managed constraints) rather than needing different screens.

PRIMARY

Dr. Arjun Mehta — Clinic OPD physician

General physician, 12 yrs, one clinic affiliation. 40–60 patients/day in 3–5 minute consults, morning + evening OPD blocks. Types with two fingers; a receptionist manages his floor. Auriva's queue is his cockpit — anything that adds a click between patients is a defect.

SECONDARY

Dr. Kavya Rao — Multi-clinic specialist

Dermatologist, 2 clinics + independent tele-practice. Different fees, different hours, different patient books per location. Her core anxiety: a booking landing at clinic A while she's at clinic B. She is why clinic-context is a first-class primitive (D8), not a filter.

TERTIARY

Dr. Suresh Iyer — Hospital-affiliated consultant

Cardiologist at a hospital that adopted Auriva org-wide. Didn't choose the platform; the org did. His schedule, room and fees are largely org-managed — his workspace must make clear what he controls vs. what the hospital controls, without burying him in admin UI.

Alternate journeys
RETURNING DOCTOR

Phone → OTP → Today

Known number with a doctor role goes straight to Today (defaultWorkspacePathForRole already does this). Multi-role accounts (doctor who is also a patient) pass through AUTH-003 Workspace Chooser, which remembers the last choice per device.

MULTI-CLINIC DOCTOR

Onboard once, affiliate many

Onboarding creates the first affiliation only. Additional clinics are added later from Practice (D8) — "Add affiliation" → search clinic → request → that org's admin approves. Identity and credentials are verified once, per-org approval is per-org.

INDEPENDENT DOCTOR

Solo practice branch

Step 2's second radio. Behind the seam this is an implicit single-member organization owned by the doctor — no separate product concept, no clinic admin to wait for; only NMC verification gates go-live. Their "practice location" is self-managed in D8.

HOSPITAL DOCTOR

Org-initiated, doctor-completed

The hospital adds the doctor from its roster (ORG-010) → doctor gets an invite SMS → phone+OTP → onboarding steps 1–3 pre-filled with whatever the org supplied, doctor confirms and owns their credentials. DEFERRED Per Q7 (§02): the org-side invite mechanics are postponed to the Org-workspace track (AUTH-006/007 family) by product decision, not left open — this side only commits the doctor-side acceptance UX shown above.

One spec ambiguity, resolved explicitly. APS-003 lists five Doctor L1 modules (Today · Schedule · Patients · Consults · Earnings) with Settings/Help as utilities; APS-006 says the Doctor sidebar has "6 flat items". This blueprint resolves the count by making Practice the sixth item — the home for SET-002 (public profile) and SET-003 (clinics & fees), promoted from buried settings to a first-class module. Rationale: for a multi-clinic doctor these aren't "settings", they're the levers of their livelihood (what patients see, what they pay, where the doctor works) — and D8 needs a stable home. Not a new screen group: Practice hosts the existing SET-002/003 IDs. If product prefers the strict 5+utilities reading, Practice folds into Settings with no other change to this document.
The Dashboard decision: there is no separate Dashboard — deliberately. The brief asks for a Dashboard module; APS-003's rule N3 says the doctor's home answers "what needs me now?", and for a doctor that answer is the queue. A stats dashboard before the queue is one click between the doctor and their first patient, 40–60 times a day. Instead, Today absorbs the dashboard's job with a Day Bar glance strip (D5). Full argument and what the Day Bar contains: section D5.

Deliverable 5 · Today

Today — Mission Control, not a dashboard

Product review confirmed the no-dashboard call and renamed the deliverable from "Dashboard" to Today — stronger product language, and it says what the screen actually is. The brief asks for "the perfect Doctor Dashboard" and warns against a generic admin dashboard; the strongest way to honor both is still not building a dashboard screen at all. But "glance strip" undersold it — review feedback is that Today should read as Mission Control: in three seconds a doctor should know the clinic they're in, the shape of the day, who's next, whether they're running behind, and have one-tap access to the four things they actually do between patients. The Day Bar spec below is revised accordingly.

WHY NO DASHBOARD

The first click is sacred

40–60 times a day the doctor returns to home base between patients. A dashboard-then-queue structure taxes every return. Landing on the queue with glanceable context taxes none. (Linear lands on issues, not a chart about issues — same logic.)

WHY NO DASHBOARD

Everything the brief lists fits in one strip

Today's appointments (count + next), waiting patients (queue stats — already shipped in the rail), current queue (the rail itself), upcoming consultations (next-up card), schedule (one click to Schedule), quick actions, notifications (bell). Nothing needs a full screen.

THE ESCAPE HATCH

Re-evaluate at Earnings + Insights

If a future doctor needs cross-clinic analytics (trends, no-show rates), that's an insights page under Earnings/Consults — a destination you visit weekly, not a landing you pass through hourly. Decision stands until that need is evidenced.

The Day Bar — anatomy (revised: Mission Control)

Two rows now, not one: a glance row (unchanged in spirit, one metric added) and a Quick Actions row beneath it — the four things a doctor actually reaches for between patients, one tap away, never a menu dig.

One doctor, many organizations

Today's storage is a single clinic per StaffProfile, so this section is the UI contract the real Organization tables will fulfil; the interim UI simply renders one affiliation card. Open questions — resolved by product review

All seven answered. None required an architecture or database change beyond what's already flagged elsewhere in this document (Q1's new skipped status is the one schema note — called out again above in D6, not new here).

#QuestionDecision
Q1Skip/re-queue rule when a called patient doesn't enter.DECIDED Reception controls queue order, not doctors. A doctor may Skip a patient temporarily; skipped patients enter a skipped state and can be recalled later — never a permanent reorder by the doctor. Implemented in D6's skip affordance and the Day Bar's rail grouping (M1).
Q2Slot model semantics.DECIDED Fixed slots for MVP — 10 / 15 / 20 minute options. The organization sets the default per affiliation; the doctor may override within org-allowed limits (a min/max the org configures, not a free-for-all). Confirms this design's standing assumption (M5) and unblocks the Running Behind indicator (D5) and Pause Booking (D5 Quick Actions), both previously gated on this.
Q3Multi-affiliation conflicts.DECIDED Auriva never allows overlapping availability across a doctor's clinics. The system detects a conflict at save time in Practice ▸ Schedule and blocks publish until the doctor resolves it — no silent double-booking, no "last save wins." DOC-004's Impact Panel (D10 wireframe) is where this surfaces.
Q4Doctor leaves a clinic.DECIDED Historical consultations stay associated with that organization permanently (the record belongs to the visit, not just the doctor). Future bookings are cancelled or reassigned per the organization's policy — Auriva doesn't force one rule; the org configures it (Org-workspace track scope). The doctor's global identity and credentials are unaffected either way. Practice ▸ Locations' "leave clinic" confirm dialog states which outcome applies before the doctor confirms.
Q5Fee governance.DECIDED Each practice location stores its own fee, independent of every other affiliation — supports independent practice and multi-org doctors cleanly with one model, no special-casing. Reflected in D7 and D8's Fees sub-section.
Q6Unverified doctors/qualifications.DECIDED A doctor can complete onboarding and use their console immediately (D2's pending-banner state is correct, kept) — but their public profile is not discoverable or bookable on the marketplace until verification completes. This resolves the D7 qualifications question the same way: unverified items can be entered, but the profile as a whole doesn't go live until the registration itself is verified — no partial public visibility.
Q7Org-invited doctor flow mechanics.DEFERRED, by design — postponed to the Organization Platform phase, as recommended. This document commits only the doctor-side acceptance UX (F1 hospital variant, D2); invite channel and pre-fill scope are that track's decision.
