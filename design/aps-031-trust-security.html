01 · Security

How we protect the platform

Every action in Auriva runs through a single, centralized authorization layer — there is no route or screen that checks permissions independently. Data is encrypted in transit and at rest as a standard commitment across the platform. When something is denied, we tell you exactly which permission was missing, instead of a generic "access denied."
Technical detail
🔐

Encryption in transit & at rest

All traffic is encrypted in transit (TLS). Data at rest is encrypted as a standard infrastructure commitment. This is a baseline, not a differentiator — we mention it because a trust page that omits it would be more suspicious than one that states it plainly.

🛡️

Role-based access control

Every permission check runs through one authorization module — no scattered, ad-hoc checks. Real platform behavior: when a user is denied an action, the system names the specific missing grant (e.g. "Billing · View"), not a generic error.

📜

Audit logging

Every appointment booked, rescheduled, or cancelled, every invoice, every payment is written to a tamper-evident, event-driven audit log — stamped with who did it and when. This is real and running in production today, not a roadmap item.

🚨

Incident response

We treat security incidents as a first-class operational concern: identify, contain, notify affected organizations, and remediate. We're not publishing a fixed SLA number here until we have the operational history to back one — we'd rather be honest about that than quote a figure we can't stand behind yet.

03 · Trust Center

What we log, and how long we keep it

The audit log is the substrate of trust in Auriva: every clinically or financially meaningful action — appointment changes, invoices, payments, record access — is written as an event, stamped with actor and timestamp, and never silently overwritten. It's exportable for your organization's own records and reviews.

04 · Data & Privacy

Whose data this is, and how it's shared

Your organization's data belongs to your organization, always. We don't sell patient data, and we don't share it beyond what your organization consents to. That's a plain commitment, not a technical feature — but it shapes every product decision we make.
Roadmap Per-patient "who can see my records" consent controls — letting an individual patient decide exactly which clinicians or staff can view specific parts of their record — are not live yet. Today, access follows your organization's role-based permissions. We're not overselling this: fine-grained patient-level consent is on our roadmap, not in production.
