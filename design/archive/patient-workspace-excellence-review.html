APS-010 · Product Excellence Review · Patient Workspace

Same journey. Premium execution.

This is not a redesign — the IA, the approved flows (AUTH-001/002/004, PAT-001/002/008/016, PRO-001, MKT-003/009/010) and the design system are unchanged. This review elevates information hierarchy, trust signals, empty states, and micro-interactions so the shipped implementation reads as a finished product, not a working prototype.

Every improvement below is graded on one question: does this help a patient understand what they need to do right now, faster and with more confidence — not "does this add a feature."

01PAT-001 · Home Dashboard

From "what features does Auriva have" to "what do I need right now."

The shipped Home already has the right bones — hero appointment card, quick actions, stats rail. The weak point is that four quick-action cards carry equal visual weight even though only one of them (Find a doctor) currently does anything, and the "complete your profile" nudge is buried at the bottom of a right rail nobody scrolls to on a good day.

Before — shipped
  • 4 quick-action cards, identical size: 1 live, 2 dashed "coming soon," 1 real link — no visual priority
  • Profile-completion nudge sits at the very bottom of the right rail
  • No signal at all when there's genuinely nothing to do (empty state is a single flat card)
  • No notification affordance anywhere in the header
After — this review
  • Primary action (Find a doctor) is visually promoted; "coming soon" actions recede into a lighter, smaller row
  • A single "Needs your attention" strip surfaces the most urgent real thing — profile completeness, or nothing
  • Notification bell added to top nav, feeding APS-010 §09
  • Redesigned zero-state: warm, specific, one CTA — not a dead page

02PAT-008 · Health Records

A Health Summary strip, before the timeline — not a government form.

Today the tab bar drops straight into Timeline with no orientation. Apple Health and every premium health product leads with a scannable summary. The trap to avoid: a dense label/value grid that reads clinical and cold. The fix is icon-forward tiles with the same visual language as the Home stat tiles — quiet, not bureaucratic.

Before
  • Tabs → straight to Timeline, no orientation
  • Blood group only visible on Profile, not here
  • No sense of "what's on file about me" at a glance
After
  • 8-tile Health Summary strip above the tabs
  • Empty fields (allergies, conditions) get a soft "+ Add" prompt, not a blank
  • Primary Doctor tile derived from real care-network data

03PAT-007 / MKT-002 · Doctor Discovery

Choose a doctor in under 30 seconds.

The checkbox filter sidebar from the first draft is accurate but slow to scan. Specialty chips at the top let a patient filter with one glance and one click; a proper sort control and a two-column result grid let more options be compared side-by-side instead of forcing a long single-column scroll.

Before
  • Checkbox list sidebar — scans slowly
  • Sort shown as plain text, not a real control
  • Single-column card list forces scrolling to compare
After
  • Specialty quick-filter chips, horizontal, one tap
  • Real sort dropdown: Nearest / Soonest / Top rated
  • 2-column grid on desktop — compare at a glance

05MKT-009/010 · Booking Experience

Slots grouped by time of day. Confirmation that feels finished, not just successful.

On confirmation, a 3-step "what happens next" list replaces two lonely buttons — it closes the loop instead of leaving the patient wondering if anything happens next.

A panel, not a page.

11System-wide · Micro-interactions

The product should feel alive — quietly.

MomentRule
HoverInteractive cards lift 0 → -2px with a border shift to primary/40, 150ms ease-out. Never a shadow-only hover — border + lift together read as "clickable" faster.
Focus3px teal ring (--ring) on every interactive element, no exceptions — keyboard users get the identical affordance as mouse hover.
Tab / view transitions180ms fade+8px slide, matches the existing AnimatePresence pattern already used on the auth steps — extend the same timing to Health Records tab switches and the Care detail panel.
LoadingSkeleton for content that's about to appear (cards, lists); spinner only for an in-flight action (booking submit, cancel). Never a blank screen — this rule is already followed correctly in the shipped app; keep it.
SuccessToast (existing pattern) + for the single highest-stakes success (booking confirmed), the dedicated confirmation screen gets one deliberate checkmark draw-in, 200ms, once.
ErrorInline, next to the control that failed, in plain language with a retry — never only a toast for something the patient needs to act on (e.g. a failed cancellation should show retry directly on the card, not just a passing toast).
